# Sovereign Ash: Nareth Protocol — Narrative Bible

## 1. Setting: Nareth

**Planet:** Tidally locked industrial colony
**Key Geography:**
- **Night Hemisphere:** Frozen, geothermal cities clustered around heat sources
- **Day Hemisphere:** Furnace-bright glass desert, solar infrastructure
- **Twilight Band:** Habitable zone, major settlements, the Skyhook
- **Cinder:** Shattered moon feeding a bright orbital debris ring

**Critical Infrastructure:**
- **Skyhook:** Space elevator (twilight band → orbit)
- **Crown Array:** Orbital network controlling weather mirrors, navigation, mass-driver defenses
- **Geothermal Cities:** Night-side survival depends on heat extraction
- **Mass Drivers:** Mineral export and planetary defense

**Economy:** Geothermal energy, orbital manufacturing, mineral extraction, weather management

## 2. Central Conflict

The Helix Directorate seizes the Crown Array during a failed emergency transition and declares permanent military rule under Marshal Oren Voss. The player commands Ash Unit, a civilian rescue-and-salvage group forced into combat after their relief carrier is destroyed.

**Theme:** "What is a victory worth if the world you save can no longer live with it?"

**Player Position:** NOT royalty. NOT reclaiming a throne. NO familial relationship with the antagonist. The player is a competent professional thrust into an impossible situation.

## 3. Characters

### 3.1 Player Character — Commander / ASH ONE
- Customizable appearance
- One of several original voice profiles
- Professional rescue/salvage operator
- Motivated by crew survival, not revenge or destiny

### 3.2 Dr. Amara Sen — Mission Control
- **Role:** Systems engineer, mission coordinator
- **Age:** Early 40s
- **Voice:** Low-mid register, precise international English, calm technical authority
- **Character:** Restrained warmth, emotionally honest, leads through competence
- **Arc:** From professional distance to personal investment in the squad's survival
- **Forbidden:** Theatrical announcer tone, melodrama

### 3.3 Tomas Rell — Veteran Heavy-Frame Pilot
- **Role:** Squad heavy, tactical advisor
- **Age:** Late 40s
- **Voice:** Textured baritone, dry humor, slightly tired
- **Character:** Protective, compact phrasing, survivor guilt from prior unit loss
- **Arc:** From guarded veteran to trusting the new squad
- **Forbidden:** Gravelly action-hero caricature

### 3.4 Iona Kest — Reconnaissance Pilot
- **Role:** Scout, sensor specialist
- **Age:** Late 20s
- **Voice:** Agile bright voice, fast analytical cadence, nervous humor
- **Character:** Brilliant, impulsive, brave but not invulnerable
- **Arc:** From reckless confidence to earned wisdom
- **Forbidden:** Cartoon energy, manic pixie

### 3.5 Chief Nadi Okafor — Salvage Master
- **Role:** Logistics, repairs, salvage operations
- **Age:** Mid 50s
- **Voice:** Warm contralto, practical, blunt
- **Character:** Mother-hen energy without sentimentality, impossible-request honesty
- **Arc:** From "we can't afford this" to "we can't afford NOT to"
- **Forbidden:** Magical negro trope, infinite patience

### 3.6 Lieutenant Cael Marr — Defector
- **Role:** Former Directorate officer, tactical intelligence
- **Age:** Mid 30s
- **Voice:** Measured mid register, formal diction softening over time
- **Character:** Carries moral injury, pauses before personal revelations
- **Arc:** From distrusted asset to earned ally (or not, depending on player)
- **Forbidden:** Villainous menace, stereotyped accent

### 3.7 MIRA-7 — Onboard Diagnostic Intelligence
- **Role:** Walker diagnostic system, gradual personality emergence
- **Voice:** Feminine synthetic, near-human clarity, narrow initial emotional range
- **Character:** Concise, non-human cadence, gradually reveals unauthorized memories
- **Arc:** From tool to entity with questions about its own continuity
- **Forbidden:** Generic robot monotone, metallic vocoder, seductive AI trope

### 3.8 Marshal Oren Voss — Antagonist
- **Role:** Helix Directorate military leader
- **Age:** Early 50s
- **Voice:** Controlled resonant tenor-baritone, educated diction, quiet confidence
- **Character:** Intelligent, persuasive, responsible for atrocities, believes centralized machine governance prevents collapse
- **Arc:** Never redeemed, but never cartoonish. Anger = reduced volume + sharper precision
- **Forbidden:** Shouting villain, mustache-twirling, celebrity resemblance

## 4. Dialogue Principles

### 4.1 Style
- Contemporary military clarity without franchise phrasing
- No jargon walls — technical terms explained through context
- Characters interrupt, overlap, trail off
- Radio protocol when tactical, natural when safe
- Humor under stress (realistic, not quippy)

### 4.2 Barks and Callouts
- Short (< 3 seconds)
- Information-dense
- Character-voiced even in brevity
- Priority system: Critical > Tactical > Flavor

### 4.3 Forbidden Dialogue Patterns
- "It's quiet... too quiet"
- "I've got a bad feeling about this"
- Catchphrase repetition
- Exposition dumps disguised as conversation
- Any line recognizable from existing franchises

## 5. Campaign Narrative Arc

### Act I — Forced Into Fire (Operations I–II)
- Ash Unit's relief carrier destroyed
- Survival becomes resistance
- Learning to fight with rescue equipment
- Establishing the squad dynamic

### Act II — The Cost of Fighting (Operations III–V)
- Growing capability, growing stakes
- Civilian cost becomes visible
- Squad relationships deepen under pressure
- Voss's logic becomes harder to dismiss

### Act III — The Choice (Operation VI)
- THE SPLIT: Save arcology OR seize orbital key
- No "good" choice — both have catastrophic cost
- Consequences ripple through final operation

### Act IV — Sovereign Ash (Operation VII)
- Combined-arms assault on Skyhook
- Final confrontation with Voss
- Crown Array fate: destroy or control
- Ending states based on accumulated choices

## 6. Ending Variables

The final state depends on:
1. Branch choice (Arcology vs. Orbital Key)
2. Optional evidence recovered
3. Civilian survival rate
4. Squadmate survival
5. Captured vs. destroyed infrastructure
6. Crown Array: destroyed or controlled

Minimum 4 distinct ending states, no "secret best ending."

## 7. World-Building Rules

- Technology is industrial, not magical
- No FTL, no teleportation, no energy shields (physical armor only)
- AI exists but is narrow (MIRA-7 is exceptional, not typical)
- No aliens — human colony, human conflict
- Economy and logistics matter (ammo, fuel, parts)
- Civilians are present and vulnerable
- War has cost beyond the battlefield
